# 1. LLM Fundamentals

### 1. Why do LLMs need huge training data?

**Keywords:** `Billions of tokens` · `language understanding` · `word relationships` · `large models`

**Explanation:**
LLMs need a huge amount of data to learn language patterns, relationships between words, and more complex concepts.

---

### 2. What are the main stages of LLM training?

**Keywords:** `Pre-train` · `Fine-tune` · `RLHF`

**Explanation:**
First the model learns from massive data, then it is fine-tuned for specific tasks, and finally RLHF improves the quality of its answers.

---

### 3. Why can LLMs answer situations not in the training data?

**Keywords:** `not memorization` · `learn patterns` · `generalization`

**Explanation:**
LLMs don't only memorize training data. They learn patterns and can combine that knowledge to generate new answers.

---

### 4. Why do LLMs hallucinate?

**Keywords:** `don't know true/false` · `most likely tokens` · `wrong information`

**Explanation:**
An LLM predicts the most likely next token rather than checking whether information is actually true, so it can produce incorrect information.

---

### 5. How does context size affect an LLM's memory?

**Keywords:** `max tokens` · `forget earlier turns` · `long books/code`

**Explanation:**
Context size is the maximum number of tokens the model can process in one context. A larger context allows it to handle longer conversations, books, or code.

---

### 6. Why are LLMs often weak at math?

**Keywords:** `tokenizer` · `next token` · `exact calculation`

**Explanation:**
LLMs predict tokens and are optimized for language, not exact mathematical calculation.

---

### 7. How does LLM size affect capability?

**Keywords:** `parameter count` · `billion params` · `complex tasks`

**Explanation:**
Model size refers to the number of parameters. Larger models generally have more learning capacity for complex tasks.

---

### 8. How does knowledge cutoff affect accuracy?

**Keywords:** `train date` · `no awareness` · `stale information`

**Explanation:**
The model's knowledge is limited by its training data. Information after the training date may be missing or outdated.

---

# 2. Prompt Engineering

### 9. What is Prompt Engineering?

**Keywords:** `crafting/tuning prompts` · `effective LLM interaction` · `better results`

**Explanation:**
Prompt Engineering means designing and tuning prompts so the LLM better understands the intent and produces useful results.

---

### 10. How do you write a clear, effective prompt?

**Keywords:** `enough context` · `enough info` · `avoid vague asks`

**Explanation:**
Give the model enough context and information. Avoid vague requests so the model clearly understands the task.

---

### 11. How do you turn a basic prompt into a complete one?

**Keywords:** `content` · `length` · `audience` · `style`

**Explanation:**
Add details such as what content is needed, expected length, target audience, and writing style.

---

### 12. What does role-playing do in Prompt Engineering?

**Keywords:** `role` · `expert` · `tone` · `approach`

**Explanation:**
Giving the LLM a role, such as developer or teacher, can change its tone and approach to match that role.

---

### 13. Why separate context/input data from instructions?

**Keywords:** `separate instruction vs data` · `less confusion` · `more accurate`

**Explanation:**
Separating instructions from input data makes the prompt clearer and reduces confusion for the model.

---

### 14. Zero-shot vs One-shot vs Few-shot?

**Keywords:** `no examples` · `1 example` · `many examples`

**Explanation:**
Zero-shot gives no examples, One-shot gives one example, and Few-shot provides multiple examples to guide the model.

---

### 15. Why specify output length and format?

**Keywords:** `control length` · `clear format` · `reuse output`

**Explanation:**
Specifying the format and length makes the response easier to organize, process, and reuse.

---

### 16. Why prefer affirmative instructions over negations?

**Keywords:** `next-token prediction` · `negatives harder` · `say what to do`

**Explanation:**
Because LLMs predict the next token, positive instructions are generally easier for them to follow than instructions focused on what not to do.

---

# 3. Tokens & Context

### 17. Why do LLMs predict tokens instead of words?

**Keywords:** `ML models` · `numbers` · `token IDs`

**Explanation:**
Machine-learning models work with numbers. A tokenizer converts text into token IDs that the model can process.

---

### 18. Character vs Word vs Subword tokenizer?

**Keywords:** `Character` · `Word` · `Subword (BPE)` · `best balance`

**Explanation:**
Character tokenization creates many tokens, word tokenization struggles with rare words, while Subword/BPE provides a useful balance.

---

### 19. What is context size?

**Keywords:** `max tokens` · `short-term memory` · `long text`

**Explanation:**
Context size is the maximum number of tokens the model can process in a prompt, limiting how much long text it can handle.

---

### 20. How do you handle content exceeding context size?

**Keywords:** `drop early tokens` · `summarize` · `RAG`

**Explanation:**
You can remove older content, summarize important information, or use RAG to retrieve only the relevant information when needed.

---

# 4. LLM API & Security

### 21. Why never share an API key?

**Keywords:** `auth credential` · `account` · `credit` · `public GitHub`

**Explanation:**
An API key is an authentication credential. If leaked, someone can use your account and consume your credit.

---

### 22. How do you protect API keys in web/mobile apps?

**Keywords:** `never store on client` · `backend` · `auth user`

**Explanation:**
Keep the API key on the backend. The client should call your backend instead of directly accessing the LLM provider.

---

### 23. Why use `stream=True`?

**Keywords:** `chunked tokens` · `output immediately` · `better UX`

**Explanation:**
Streaming sends the response in chunks, allowing users to see the answer immediately instead of waiting for the entire response.

---

### 24. How do you build a long conversation via API?

**Keywords:** `messages array` · `system` · `user` · `assistant`

**Explanation:**
Keep conversation history in the messages array using the `system`, `user`, and `assistant` roles.

---

### 25. How do you track token usage?

**Keywords:** `prompt_tokens` · `completion_tokens` · `total_tokens`

**Explanation:**
The API response provides token usage information. You can use it to monitor consumption and control cost.

---

### 26. How does `max_tokens` affect output?

**Keywords:** `max answer length` · `stop` · `token limit`

**Explanation:**
`max_tokens` controls the maximum length of the generated answer. If it is too low, the response can be cut off.

---

# 5. Function Calling

### 27. Why do LLMs need Function Calling?

**Keywords:** `knowledge limits` · `external world` · `APIs` · `tools`

**Explanation:**
LLMs cannot directly interact with the external world. Function Calling allows them to use APIs, tools, and external systems.

---

### 28. How does Function Calling work?

**Keywords:** `analyzes intent` · `picks function` · `executes` · `result`

**Explanation:**
The LLM analyzes the user's intent, chooses a function and arguments, the application executes it, and the result is returned to the LLM.

---

### 29. What are real-world Function Calling use cases?

**Keywords:** `weather` · `scheduling` · `online purchase` · `external APIs`

**Explanation:**
Function Calling can connect an LLM to real-time services such as weather, scheduling, purchases, and other APIs.

---

### 30. What are the main parts of a function definition?

**Keywords:** `name` · `description` · `parameters`

**Explanation:**
A function definition tells the LLM what the function is called, what it does, and what parameters it accepts.

---

### 31. How do you handle Function Calling results?

**Keywords:** `content` · `tool_calls` · `arguments` · `json.loads()`

**Explanation:**
Check whether the response contains normal content or tool calls. If there is a tool call, extract the arguments, parse them, and execute the matching function.

---

### 32. How do you optimize Function Calling?

**Keywords:** `clear names` · `limit functions` · `enums` · `system prompt`

**Explanation:**
Use clear function and argument names, limit the number of available functions, use enums where appropriate, and give the model guidance in the system prompt.

---

# 6. Embeddings & Vector DB

### 33. How do embeddings capture semantic similarity?

**Keywords:** `text` · `numeric vectors` · `cosine similarity`

**Explanation:**
Embeddings convert text into numeric vectors. Text with similar meanings tends to have similar vectors, which can be compared using cosine similarity.

---

### 34. Why are embeddings important?

**Keywords:** `text/image/audio` · `vectors` · `search` · `recommendations`

**Explanation:**
Embeddings turn complex data into vectors that can be used for search, recommendations, clustering, and other AI applications.

---

### 35. Vector DB vs traditional DB?

**Keywords:** `embedding vectors` · `ANN search` · `fast retrieval`

**Explanation:**
Vector databases are designed to store and efficiently search embedding vectors using methods such as ANN search.

---

### 36. Why does vector similarity get hard as data grows?

**Keywords:** `cosine` · `once per vector` · `heavy compute`

**Explanation:**
Comparing a query against every vector becomes expensive as the number of vectors increases, so efficient indexing becomes important.

---

### 37. What are key Vector DB features?

**Keywords:** `store` · `index` · `ANN search` · `scalability`

**Explanation:**
A Vector DB should efficiently store vectors, provide fast approximate nearest-neighbor search, and scale with the dataset.

---

# 7. RAG

### 38. What LLM limits does RAG solve?

**Keywords:** `update knowledge` · `higher accuracy` · `trusted sources` · `no retraining`

**Explanation:**
RAG allows an application to retrieve updated and trusted information without retraining the model.

---

### 39. What are the basic RAG flow steps?

**Keywords:** `prepare data` · `embed question` · `retrieve chunks` · `LLM`

**Explanation:**
Prepare and embed the data, embed the user's question, retrieve relevant chunks, and give those chunks to the LLM to generate the answer.

---

### 40. How does RAG help a product advisory chatbot?

**Keywords:** `product info` · `LLM prompt` · `product facts` · `no retraining`

**Explanation:**
The chatbot retrieves relevant product information and puts it into the LLM prompt, allowing answers based on current product facts.

---

### 41. Is a Vector DB required for RAG?

**Keywords:** `not mandatory` · `alternatives` · `keyword DB query`

**Explanation:**
A Vector DB is useful but not mandatory. RAG can also retrieve information through other approaches, such as keyword database queries.

---

### 42. Why use RAG when context windows are huge?

**Keywords:** `accuracy` · `security` · `cost/speed`

**Explanation:**
Sending everything to the LLM can still create accuracy, security, cost, and speed problems. RAG retrieves only relevant information.

---

### 43. RAG vs Function Calling?

**Keywords:** `Function Calling` → `structured tasks` · `APIs`

**Keywords:** `RAG` → `updated knowledge` · `citations`

**Explanation:**
Function Calling is mainly for performing structured actions through APIs, while RAG is mainly for retrieving relevant and updated knowledge.

---

### 44. How combine RAG and Function Calling?

**Keywords:** `pick action` · `fetch context` · `final answer`

**Explanation:**
The LLM can choose an action with Function Calling, retrieve relevant information through RAG, and then use that context to produce the final answer.

---

### 45. What are the main challenges of basic RAG?

**Keywords:** `irrelevant retrieval` · `duplicate/noisy info` · `slow`

**Explanation:**
A basic RAG system can retrieve irrelevant or noisy information and may become slow without proper filtering and ranking.

---

# 8. Chunking

### 46. Why is chunking critical in RAG?

**Keywords:** `chunk size` · `accuracy` · `context`

**Explanation:**
Chunk size affects retrieval accuracy. Chunks need to be small enough for efficient processing but large enough to preserve useful context.

---

### 47. How do you choose chunk size?

**Keywords:** `small enough` · `large enough` · `data type` · `context window`

**Explanation:**
The right chunk size depends on the data type and context window. It needs to balance retrieval precision with enough context.

---

### 48. How do `chunk_size` and `chunk_overlap` affect results?

**Keywords:** `max chunk length` · `continuity` · `search quality`

**Explanation:**
`chunk_size` controls the maximum chunk length, while `chunk_overlap` keeps continuity between chunks and can affect search quality.

---

# 9. Production

### 49. How do you optimize LLM operating cost?

**Keywords:** `right-sized model` · `response caching` · `rate limits`

**Explanation:**
Use an appropriate model for the task, reuse responses when possible, and limit excessive requests to control operating cost.

---

### 50. How do you protect an LLM application in production?

**Keywords:** `input validation` · `sensitive context` · `layered defense` · `content moderation`

**Explanation:**
Validate inputs, minimize sensitive information in the context, use multiple security layers, and apply content moderation.
