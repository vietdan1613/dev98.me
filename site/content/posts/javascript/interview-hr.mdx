## Introduction and Motivation

### 1. Tell me about yourself. / Can you introduce yourself?

**Keywords:** `who you are` · `role` · `tech stack` · `frontend / full stack` · `key projects` · `growth goals`

**Expectations:** Introduce who you are, your current role, and the core skills that match the job. Cover your main technologies, whether your strength is frontend or full stack, a few notable projects, and what you want to grow into next.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 2. What do you know about our company?

**Keywords:** `product` · `domain` · `why it interests you`

**Expectations:** Show that you understand the company’s product or domain, then explain why it interests you.

---

### 3. What interests you about this position? / Why do you want to join us?

**Keywords:** `role fit` · `skills` · `growth` · `product` · `stack`

**Expectations:** Connect the role to your skills and the growth you want next. For a product like TaloTrace, mention interest in AI-supported software testing, building with React and Next.js, and working in a fast-paced environment with ownership of product quality.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 4. Where do you see yourself in five years?

**Keywords:** `impact` · `growth` · `engineer`

**Expectations:** Describe your aim to become a more impactful engineer with deeper ownership and stronger technical judgment.

---

### 5. What are your salary expectations?

**Keywords:** `open discussion` · `market` · `role scope`

**Expectations:** Stay open to discussion and frame your expectations around market range and role scope.

---

## Experience and Projects

### 6. Tell me about a project you have built and explain your role.

**Keywords:** `problem` · `role` · `tech` · `ownership` · `challenge` · `result`

**Expectations:** Walk through Project → Role → Problem → Solution → Tech → Result. Explain the problem the project solved, the technologies used, the parts you owned, the biggest difficulty, and the outcome.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 7. Tell us about a piece of work you are proud of. What was your part in it?

**Keywords:** `personal contribution` · `checkable result` · `reflection`

**Expectations:** Name concrete work, state your personal contribution clearly, and share a result someone could verify. Strong answers also include a measured result and what you would do differently.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** Cannot name work, or only describes the team with no personal part.
- **2:** Names work, but the personal contribution stays vague.
- **3:** Clear personal contribution and a checkable result.
- **4:** Clear contribution, a measured result, and what they would do differently.

---

### 8. Can you walk me through your coding challenge solution?

**Keywords:** `requirements` · `architecture` · `key features` · `testing` · `limitations`

**Expectations:** Prepare this carefully when the process includes a take-home. Cover the main requirements, architecture, important features, how you tested, remaining limitations, and how you would improve them.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 9. If you had two more days to improve your coding challenge, what would you do?

**Keywords:** `missing tests` · `error handling` · `performance` · `simpler structure`

**Expectations:** Name 2–3 high-value improvements that are real gaps in your submission. Only mention improvements that apply to your actual solution.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

## Working Style and Soft Skills

### 10. When you get stuck, how do you proceed?

**Keywords:** `break down` · `research` · `ask for help`

**Expectations:** Explain that you break the problem down and research first, then ask for help with a clear summary of what you tried.

---

### 11. How do you learn and improve your skills?

**Keywords:** `practice` · `documentation` · `people`

**Expectations:** Mention deliberate practice, reading documentation, and learning from teammates.

---

### 12. What have you learned recently, and how did you learn it?

**Keywords:** `specific skill` · `how learned` · `where used` · `measured effect`

**Expectations:** Name a specific skill, how you learned it, and where you used it. Strong answers also show a deliberate learning method and a measured effect on your work.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** Nothing specific.
- **2:** Names a topic but not how it was learned or used.
- **3:** A specific skill, how it was learned, and where it was used.
- **4:** A specific skill, a deliberate learning method, and a measured effect on their work.

---

### 13. How do you handle a challenging feature?

**Keywords:** `requirements` · `split tasks` · `incremental`

**Expectations:** Clarify requirements, split the work into small tasks, and deliver incrementally.

---

### 14. How do you handle stress and pressure?

**Keywords:** `stay calm` · `prioritize` · `break down`

**Expectations:** Stay calm, prioritize what matters most, and break large pressure into smaller steps.

---

### 15. What are your strengths?

**Keywords:** `problem-solving` · `responsibility` · `willingness to learn`

**Expectations:** Highlight strengths such as problem-solving, ownership, and willingness to learn.

---

### 16. What are your weaknesses?

**Keywords:** `honest` · `improving`

**Expectations:** Be honest and name a real area, then show how you are improving it.

---

### 17. What is your preferred working or management style?

**Keywords:** `collaboration` · `feedback` · `teamwork`

**Expectations:** Prefer collaboration, clear feedback, and working as a team toward shared goals.

---

### 18. Have you mentored juniors? What is your leadership style?

**Keywords:** `mentor` · `explain` · `guide` · `review` · `feedback`

**Expectations:** Lead by mentoring: explain, guide, review work, and give actionable feedback.

---

### 19. Tell us about a disagreement with a colleague. How did you resolve it?

**Keywords:** `both views` · `listen` · `resolve` · `relationship after`

**Expectations:** Explain both views, how you listened, and how it was resolved. Strong answers also show where you changed your mind when fair and how the working relationship looked afterward.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** Blames the colleague, or says they never disagree with anyone.
- **2:** Describes the disagreement but not how it ended.
- **3:** Explains both views, how they listened, and how it was resolved.
- **4:** Explains both views, changed their own mind where fair, and the working relationship after.

---

### 20. Tell us about a mistake you made at work. What did you learn?

**Keywords:** `own it` · `how found` · `specific lesson` · `process change`

**Expectations:** Own a real mistake, explain how it was found, and share a specific lesson. Strong answers also cover how damage was contained and a concrete change to how you work now.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** No real mistake, or the mistake was someone else's.
- **2:** A real mistake, but the lesson is generic.
- **3:** Owns the mistake, how it was found, and a specific lesson.
- **4:** Owns it, how the damage was contained, and a concrete change to how they work now.

## Tools and Delivery Process

### 21. What tools do you use to manage your work?

**Keywords:** `VS Code` · `Cursor` · `Git` · `Vitest` · `Jira` · `Trello`

**Expectations:** Name your common stack: an editor (VS Code / Cursor), Git, tests (Vitest), and a tracker (Jira / Trello).

---

### 22. How do you plan and organize a new project?

**Keywords:** `requirements` · `plan tasks` · `priorities` · `milestones`

**Expectations:** Clarify requirements, plan tasks, then set priorities and milestones so you finish on time.

---

### 23. Can you describe your CI/CD experience?

**Keywords:** `Git` · `GitHub Actions` · `CI` · `Vercel` · `CD`

**Expectations:** Walk through your typical flow: Git repository → GitHub Actions for CI → Vercel (or similar) for CD.

---

### 24. Walk through your software development process.

**Keywords:** `requirements` · `planning` · `design` · `development` · `testing` · `deployment` · `maintenance`

**Expectations:** Cover each stage in order: Requirements → Planning → Design → Development → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance.

---

### 25. What is Agile methodology?

**Keywords:** `small iterations` · `collaboration` · `feedback` · `adapt` · `deliver value`

**Expectations:** Agile delivers value in small iterations through team collaboration, continuous feedback, and adapting to change.

---

## AI-Assisted Development

Prioritize this group when the job description emphasizes daily use of AI coding agents.

### 26. How do you use AI coding tools in your daily development workflow?

**Keywords:** `requirements` · `planning` · `small increments` · `review` · `test / fix` · `you own the code`

**Expectations:** Describe a real workflow: analyze requirements, plan with AI, implement in small pieces, review the code, run tests, and fix issues. Emphasize that AI speeds you up, but you remain responsible for the final code.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 27. How do you verify AI-generated code before accepting it?

**Keywords:** `diff review` · `lint / type-check / tests` · `edge cases` · `manual check`

**Expectations:** Check the logic and diff, run lint, type-check, and relevant tests, consider edge cases, try the feature yourself, and never claim the work is finished without verification.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 28. Tell me about a time an AI coding tool produced incorrect code. How did you handle it?

**Keywords:** `what went wrong` · `how you found it` · `fix` · `verification`

**Expectations:** Use a real example. Cover what the AI got wrong, how you discovered it, whether you fixed it yourself or guided the AI, and how you verified the fix.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 29. How do you balance development speed with code quality when using AI?

**Keywords:** `highest-value first` · `small testable slices` · `simple solutions` · `do not skip tests` · `iterate`

**Expectations:** Prioritize the most important parts first, break work into small testable pieces, reuse simple suitable solutions, do not skip testing just to finish faster, and improve weaker parts in later passes.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

## Frontend and Next.js

### 30. How do you turn a Figma design into a production-ready, pixel-accurate UI?

**Keywords:** `layout / spacing / typography / tokens` · `reusable components` · `React / Next.js` · `responsive / states` · `compare to design`

**Expectations:** Analyze layout, spacing, typography, and design tokens; identify reusable components; build with React/Next.js; handle responsive and interactive states; then compare the result with Figma and adjust.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 31. How do you structure a React or Next.js application to keep it maintainable?

**Keywords:** `feature-based` · `routing / UI / logic` · `clear ownership` · `avoid early abstraction` · `server vs client`

**Expectations:** Use a feature-based structure when it fits, separate routing, UI, and business logic, keep components focused, reuse code without over-abstracting early, and keep clear server/client boundaries.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 32. How do you handle loading, error, and empty states in a React application?

**Keywords:** `loading` · `API errors` · `empty data` · `invalid user actions`

**Expectations:** Explain how users always get appropriate feedback while data is loading, when an API fails, when there is no data, and when the user takes an invalid action.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 33. How would you investigate and improve the performance of a slow Next.js application?

**Keywords:** `measure first` · `network / API` · `rendering / bundle` · `fetching / caching / images` · `measure again`

**Expectations:** Measure before optimizing, inspect network requests and API latency, analyze rendering and bundle size, optimize data fetching, caching, or images when relevant, then measure again to confirm the improvement.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

## Full-Stack, Testing, and Debugging

### 34. How do you design and implement a full-stack feature from frontend to backend?

**Keywords:** `requirements / UX flow` · `API / data model` · `backend + validation` · `frontend integration` · `states / edge cases` · `tests`

**Expectations:** Clarify requirements and user flow, design the API and data shape, build backend and validation, integrate the frontend, handle loading/errors/edge cases, then write tests and verify the full path.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 35. What were the biggest technical challenges you faced, and how did you solve them?

**Keywords:** `specific problem` · `root cause` · `options considered` · `result`

**Expectations:** Pick one concrete issue. Explain how you found the cause, which options you considered, what you chose, and what the result was.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 36. How do you investigate a difficult bug or a production issue?

**Keywords:** `reproduce` · `logs / evidence` · `narrow scope` · `root cause` · `fix + regression test` · `recheck`

**Expectations:** Reproduce the issue, gather logs and evidence, narrow the cause, identify the root cause, fix it and add a regression test, then verify so it does not come back.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

---

### 37. How do you ensure your code is correct, maintainable, and production-ready?

**Keywords:** `code review` · `lint / type-check` · `tests` · `error handling` · `manual verification`

**Expectations:** Mention code review, linting and type-checking, unit/integration/E2E tests as appropriate, error and edge-case handling, and real verification before shipping.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

### 38. What is your testing strategy for a new feature?

**Keywords:** `unit` · `integration` · `E2E for critical flows` · `error cases` · `risk-based coverage`

**Expectations:** Use unit tests for small logic, integration tests for interacting parts, E2E for critical flows, cover failure cases, and re-run tests after changes. Focus on choosing the right level of testing for the risk rather than claiming 100% line coverage.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

---

## Deadlines and Prioritization

### 39. Describe a time you had to meet a tight deadline. What did you do?

**Keywords:** `STAR` · `prioritize` · `cut or delegate` · `trade-off` · `result` · `prevent a repeat`

**Expectations:** Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Show specific actions, including what you cut or delegated, and the outcome. Strong answers also include a deliberate trade-off and how you prevent a repeat. Focus on the critical path, not only working longer hours.

⏱️ Suggested length: 60–90 seconds.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** No real example, or the deadline was simply missed with no reflection.
- **2:** An example, but the actions are generic, such as working late.
- **3:** Specific actions taken, including what was cut or delegated, and the outcome.
- **4:** Specific actions, a trade-off made on purpose, the outcome, and how they prevent a repeat.

---

### 40. How do you prioritize tasks when everything feels urgent?

**Keywords:** `user impact` · `urgency / risk` · `dependencies` · `implementation cost` · `highest value soonest` · `align early`

**Expectations:** Rank work by user impact, urgency and risk, dependencies, and implementation cost. Choose the work that creates the most value fastest. If deadline and scope conflict, communicate with stakeholders early about what waits.

⏱️ Suggested length: 45–60 seconds.

**Score yourself (1–4):**

- **1:** No method, or whoever shouts loudest.
- **2:** A general method with no example.
- **3:** A clear way of ranking work, with a real example.
- **4:** A clear way of ranking by impact, a real example, and how they tell others what waits.
